Copyright and DMCA Takedown Policy (draft)

Draft prepared with AI assistance; not legal advice; have a qualified lawyer review before publishing.

RLabs ("we", "us"). App: NoAdReader for Android. Last updated: 30 October 2026.

1. Who we are and what the app does

NoAdReader ("the app") is published by RLabs, KR Puram, Bengaluru, Karnataka 560036, India ("we"). The app is a reader that works only on your device. It opens files you choose from your own storage and keeps your highlights, notes and reading progress on your device.

We do not host, store, index, link to, sell or distribute books or other content. The app comes with no books. It has no internet access, no accounts and no servers. We cannot see, access or remove the files on users' devices.

We respect the rights of authors and publishers, and we expect our users to do the same. Users must only open files they have the right to use (see our Terms of Service).

2. What you can report to us

Because we don't hold users' files, we cannot delete or block a file stored on someone's phone, and we cannot identify who has a particular file. A notice about content on a user's own device can't be acted on by us.

You can report to us, and we will act on:

  • material that we publish: the app itself (its code, fonts, icons, images and text), our app-store listings and screenshots, our website and help pages;
  • in the future, any content stored on or shown from our systems, if we add such features (we will update this policy first).

If you think a copy of your work is being shared publicly, the right place to report it is the website, cloud service or platform that hosts it. For complaints about the app's listing you can also use the app store's own copyright complaint form.

3. How to send a copyright notice (United States, 17 U.S.C. §512(c)(3))

Send a written notice to our designated agent (section 7). To be effective, it must include:

  1. A physical or electronic signature of a person authorised to act for the owner of the exclusive right that is allegedly infringed.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to be infringed (or, if several works are covered by one notice, a representative list).
  3. Identification of the material claimed to be infringing and information reasonably sufficient to let us locate it (for example the URL of the store listing or web page, or a description of the asset in the app).
  4. Your contact information: name, address, telephone number and, if available, email address.
  5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use of the material in the manner complained of is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
  6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate, and, under penalty of perjury, that you are authorised to act on behalf of the owner of the exclusive right.

Under 17 U.S.C. §512(f), anyone who knowingly and materially misrepresents that material is infringing may be liable for damages. Please consider whether the use could be licensed, fair use, or otherwise permitted before you send a notice.

4. Counter-notice (17 U.S.C. §512(g)(3))

If we remove material that we published or that a user stored on our systems, and the affected person believes this was a mistake or misidentification, they may send a counter-notice to our designated agent containing:

  1. Their physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the material that was removed or disabled and the location where it appeared before removal.
  3. A statement under penalty of perjury that they have a good-faith belief that the material was removed or disabled as a result of mistake or misidentification.
  4. Their name, address and telephone number, and a statement that they consent to the jurisdiction of the US Federal District Court for the judicial district in which their address is located (or, if their address is outside the United States, any judicial district in which we may be found), and that they will accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice or that person's agent.

When we receive a valid counter-notice we will forward it to the person who sent the original notice. Unless that person tells us within 10 to 14 business days that they have filed a court action to stop the infringement, we may restore the material.

5. Notices from the European Union (Digital Services Act, Article 16) and elsewhere

If you are in the EU, or anywhere outside the United States, you can report allegedly illegal content (including copyright infringement) to the same address. Please include:

  1. a sufficiently substantiated explanation of why you consider the content illegal;
  2. a clear indication of its exact electronic location, such as the exact URL, and any further information needed to identify it;
  3. your name and email address (not required for reports about child sexual abuse material);
  4. a statement confirming your good-faith belief that the information and allegations in the notice are accurate and complete.

We will confirm receipt without undue delay, review the notice diligently and objectively, and tell you what we decided and how you can seek redress. If we remove or restrict content provided by a user, we will tell that user why (a statement of reasons) and how they can contest the decision. Nothing in this policy limits your right to go to a court or to an out-of-court dispute settlement body.

As explained in section 2, the app does not currently host user content, so in practice these notices can only concern material we publish ourselves.

6. Repeat infringers

Where we provide any service that stores or shares content for users, we will, in appropriate circumstances, suspend or end the access of users who repeatedly infringe copyright, after warning them where it is reasonable to do so. As the app currently has no accounts and no hosted content, there is no user account we could suspend; this section will apply to any such feature we add later.
